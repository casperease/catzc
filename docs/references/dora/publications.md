## 2026 ROI of AI-assisted Software Development

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/ai/roi/report/> (download:
  <https://cloud.google.com/resources/content/dora-roi-of-ai-assisted-software-development>)
- **Published:** April 2026. It is the publication featured on the [AI page](https://dora.dev/ai/) and the research page.
- **What the site says:**
  - It offers a practical framework for working through the costs and returns of AI adoption.
  - It covers managing the initial "productivity dip" of a rollout and defending an AI budget for the next fiscal year.
  - It supplies the calculations and conversation starters for both.
  - It comes with an interactive [ROI of AI calculator](https://dora.dev/ai/roi/calculator/).
- The site summarizes the publication's purpose, not its findings.

## 2025 DORA AI Capabilities Model

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/ai/capabilities-model/report/>
- **Published:** December 2025 ([2025 year in review](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-2025-year-in-review/)). It is a companion to the 2025
  State of AI-assisted Software Development.
- **Headline findings:**
  - Seven capabilities amplify the benefits of AI adoption. See [AI Capabilities Model](ai.md#ai-capabilities-model).
  - For each capability, it gives implementation strategies, tactics for getting started, and ways to monitor progress and keep improving.
  - Each of the seven has a capability page and a [survey question set](https://dora.dev/ai/capabilities-model/questions/) for
    self-assessment.

## 2025 State of AI-assisted Software Development

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/> (overview: <https://dora.dev/research/2025/>)
- **Published:** September 2025. It is the 2025 annual DORA Report, renamed from "Accelerate State of DevOps"
  ([2025 year in review](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-2025-year-in-review/)).
- **Headline findings:**
  - AI is mainly an amplifier: it magnifies the strengths of high-performing organizations and the dysfunctions of struggling ones. The
    greatest returns come from focusing on the underlying organizational system, not the tools
    ([2025 page](https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/)).
  - AI improves throughput, but often at the cost of stability when the foundations are weak
    ([2025 year in review](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-2025-year-in-review/)).
  - It introduced the DORA AI Capabilities Model's seven capabilities ([publications](https://dora.dev/research/publications/)).
  - 90% of organizations use an internal developer platform and 76% have dedicated platform teams. "Clear feedback on the outcome of my
    tasks" is the platform trait most correlated with a positive experience
    ([platform engineering](https://dora.dev/capabilities/platform-engineering/)).
  - The full version describes seven team profiles drawn from the data ([version check](https://dora.dev/vc/)). Its benchmarks now feed the
    [Quick Check](https://dora.dev/quickcheck/) ([FAQ](https://dora.dev/faq/)).
- **Also in the archive:** a [measurement frameworks](https://dora.dev/research/2025/measurement-frameworks/) article,
  [team-profile survey questions](https://dora.dev/research/2025/team-profiles/questions/), and
  [errata](https://dora.dev/research/2025/errata/). The errata's table of contents shows chapters on AI adoption, the AI Capabilities Model,
  platform engineering, value stream management, and metrics frameworks.

## 2025 Impact of Generative AI in Software Development

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/ai/gen-ai-report/report/>
- **Published:** March 2025 ([2025 year in review](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-2025-year-in-review/)). It builds on the 2024 findings.
- **Headline findings** (all from the [publication page](https://dora.dev/ai/gen-ai-report/report/)):
  - Heavy users of generative AI report more time in flow, higher job satisfaction, higher productivity, and less burnout.
  - "Valuable work" paradox: AI adoption goes with _less_ time spent on valuable work, while toil stays the same. The "vacuum hypothesis"
    explains this: AI speeds up the enjoyable, valuable tasks but has not yet removed the drudgery.
  - A 25% increase in AI adoption is associated with a 1.5% decrease in delivery throughput and a 7.2% decrease in delivery stability. One
    cause is larger batches.
  - Trust drives AI productivity, yet 39% of developers trust AI output only a little or not at all.
  - Organizational levers are associated with higher team AI adoption: addressing job-displacement fears (125% more adoption), dedicated
    learning time (131% more), and clear acceptable-use policies (451% more).
  - Fast, high-quality feedback loops (test automation, fast code review, CI) are the counterweight to AI-generated volume.

## 2024 Accelerate State of DevOps Report

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/> (overview: <https://dora.dev/research/2024/>)
- **Published:** October 2024. The research archive summarizes it as platform engineering and user-centricity driving success while AI
  begins to reshape delivery.
- **Headline findings** (from the [2024 page](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/)):
  - AI adoption raises individual productivity, flow, and job satisfaction, but lowers software delivery throughput and stability. Small
    batches and robust testing still matter.
  - User-centricity drives product quality. User-centred developers are more productive, more satisfied, and less likely to burn out.
  - Unstable organizational priorities cut productivity and raise burnout substantially, even with strong leaders and good documentation.
  - Internal developer platforms raise individual productivity, team performance, and organizational performance, but can lower throughput
    and change stability.
  - Transformational leadership raises productivity, organizational performance, and job satisfaction, and lowers burnout.
  - Flexible infrastructure raises organizational performance. Migrating to the cloud without using its flexibility can be worse than
    staying in a data centre.
- **Figures from the [2024 infographic](https://dora.dev/research/2024/2024-DORA-Report-Infographic.pdf):**
  - 76% of respondents rely on AI for some tasks, and 81% say their company has shifted resources into AI.
  - A 25% increase in AI adoption is associated with rises of 7.5% in documentation quality, 3.4% in code quality, 3.1% in code review
    speed, and 1.3% in approval speed, and a 1.8% fall in code complexity.
  - 39% report little or no trust in AI.
  - 89% use an internal developer platform. Platform users see 8% lower throughput.
  - Productivity is 5% higher when platform users can finish tasks without an enabling team, and team productivity is 6% higher with a
    dedicated platform team.
- **Metrics:** this edition added deployment rework rate and the throughput/instability framing
  ([metrics history](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-metrics-history/)).
- **Preview:** an [AI preview](https://dora.dev/research/2024/ai-preview/) (August 2024) found that only 24% of respondents trust
  AI-generated code "a lot" or "a great deal".

## 2023 Accelerate State of DevOps Report

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2023/dora-report/> (overview: <https://dora.dev/research/2023/>)
- **Headline findings** (from the [2023 page](https://dora.dev/research/2023/dora-report/)):
  - Generative cultures go with 30% higher organizational performance.
  - Teams that focus on users have 40% higher organizational performance.
  - Continuous integration, loosely coupled teams, and fast code reviews significantly improve delivery and operational performance.
  - High-quality documentation sharply increases the effect of technical capabilities on organizational performance.
  - Flexible infrastructure yields 30% higher organizational performance, while plain lift-and-shift can do harm.
  - Fair work distribution reduces burnout, but not for respondents who identify as underrepresented, who often take on more repetitive
    work.
- **Metrics:** mean time to recover became failed deployment recovery time
  ([metrics history](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-metrics-history/)).
- **Method:** one model per section instead of a single large model
  ([structural equation models](https://dora.dev/research/2023/structural-equation-models/)).

## 2022 Accelerate State of DevOps Report

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2022/dora-report/> (overview: <https://dora.dev/research/2022/>)
- **Headline findings** (from the [overview](https://dora.dev/research/2022/) and [2022 page](https://dora.dev/research/2022/dora-report/)):
  - Culture, not technology, is the biggest predictor of application-development security practices. Teams with high security practice are
    1.6× more likely to have high organizational performance, and teams with low security practice have 1.4× greater odds of high burnout.
  - High software delivery performance helps organizational performance only when operational performance (reliability) is also high.
  - Software supply-chain security controls (such as SLSA) improve delivery performance, but only where continuous integration is
    established.
  - Teams combining version control and continuous delivery are 2.5× more likely to have high delivery performance. CD, loosely coupled
    teams, version control, and CI together add up to more than the sum of their parts.
  - High-trust, low-blame cultures, team stability, and flexible work arrangements go with higher organizational performance.
  - Teams that see the need to keep improving perform better.
- **Clusters:** only three performance clusters emerged this year ([FAQ](https://dora.dev/faq/)).

## 2021 Accelerate State of DevOps Report

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2021/dora-report/> (overview: <https://dora.dev/research/2021/>)
- **Headline findings** (from the [overview](https://dora.dev/research/2021/) and [2021 page](https://dora.dev/research/2021/dora-report/)):
  - Elite performers make up 26% of teams and keep shortening their lead times.
  - SRE and DevOps are complementary. Teams that prioritize both delivery and operational excellence report the highest organizational
    performance.
  - Teams using all five cloud characteristics improve delivery, operational, and organizational performance. Multi-cloud use is rising.
  - A secure software supply chain is essential and drives performance.
  - Documentation quality was measured for the first time, and it is foundational for implementing technical practices.
  - Inclusive, generative team cultures had less burnout during the COVID-19 pandemic.
- **Metrics:** benchmarked on deployment frequency, lead time, time to restore, change failure rate, and reliability. The 2026 metrics
  history notes that calling reliability the "fifth metric" was inaccurate.

## 2020 The ROI of DevOps Transformation

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2020/>
- **Note:** DORA published no annual report in 2020. This whitepaper was published instead.
- **Headline findings** (from the [2020 page](https://dora.dev/research/2020/)):
  - Technology transformation moves IT from cost centre to value driver.
  - Elite performers gain the most.
  - Value includes avoided rework and reinvesting the savings into new features, not only cost savings.
  - Retaining and training existing staff costs less than replacing them and preserves institutional knowledge.
  - The ability to experiment often is crucial to delivering valuable features.
  - Cutting downtime and change failure rates is key to lowering costs.

## 2019 Accelerate State of DevOps Report

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2019/dora-report/> (overview: <https://dora.dev/research/2019/>)
- **Headline findings** (from the [2019 page](https://dora.dev/research/2019/dora-report/)):
  - Speed and stability go together; there is no inherent trade-off.
  - Elite performers are 24 times more likely to use all five essential cloud characteristics. The flexible infrastructure capability page
    puts the 2018 and 2019 figure at "more than 23 times".
  - Productivity improves with investment in tooling, easier information search, less technical debt, and psychological safety, which in
    turn improves work/life balance and reduces burnout.
  - DevOps scales best through communities of practice and grassroots movements rather than centres of excellence (archive summary).
  - Continuous delivery reduces the risk and cost of releases.
  - Heavyweight external change approval hurts. Peer review during development, backed by automation, works better.

## 2018 Accelerate State of DevOps Report

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2018/dora-report/> (overview: <https://dora.dev/research/2018/>)
- **Note:** the first Accelerate State of DevOps edition that DORA published independently.
- **Headline findings** (from the [2018 page](https://dora.dev/research/2018/dora-report/)):
  - The **elite** cluster was introduced. Compared with low performers, elite performers deploy 46× more often, with 2,555× faster lead
    times, 2,604× faster time to restore, and 7× lower change failure rates.
  - Software delivery and operational performance brings competitive advantage: profitability, productivity, market share, and customer
    satisfaction.
  - Teams that use the essential cloud characteristics are 23× more likely to be elite.
  - Elite performers use and expand open source more. Outsourcing whole functions hurts performance.
  - Continuous testing, monitoring and observability, database change management, and early security integration are crucial.
  - Generative culture, and autonomy grounded in trust in leaders, drive performance.
- **Metrics:** availability added as an operational measure ([metrics history](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-metrics-history/)).

## 2017 State of DevOps Report

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2017/> (with Puppet)
- **Headline findings** (from the [2017 page](https://dora.dev/research/2017/)):
  - Transformational leadership (vision, inspirational communication, intellectual stimulation, support, recognition) boosts IT and
    organizational performance.
  - High performers automate much more of their configuration management, testing, deployments, and change approvals.
  - DevOps benefits not-for-profit organizations as well as commercial ones.
  - Loosely coupled architectures and teams are crucial for continuous delivery.
  - Lean product management (small batches, customer feedback) drives organizational performance.
  - Continuous delivery lowers deployment pain. Version control, CI, trunk-based development, and test automation are essential to it.

## 2016 State of DevOps Report

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2016/> (with Puppet)
- **Headline findings** (from the [2016 page](https://dora.dev/research/2016/)):
  - Compared with low performers, high performers deploy 200× more often, with 2,555× faster lead times, 24× faster recovery, and 3× lower
    change failure rates.
  - High performers spend 22% less time on unplanned work and rework, and 29% more on new work.
  - High performers spend 50% less time remediating security issues.
  - An experimental approach to product development (small batches, visibility of workflow, customer feedback) improves IT and
    organizational performance.
  - Transformation yields cost savings that can be reinvested. This analysis was later expanded in the 2020 ROI whitepaper.

## 2015 State of DevOps Report

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2015/> (with Puppet)
- **Headline findings** (from the [2015 page](https://dora.dev/research/2015/)):
  - Compared with low performers, high performers deploy 30× more often, with 200× shorter lead times, 60× fewer failures, and 168× faster
    recovery.
  - Lean management and continuous delivery reinforce each other and reduce burnout.
  - High performance is possible on greenfield, brownfield, or legacy systems if they are architected for testability and deployability.
  - IT managers are key to linking strategy with execution, and more diverse teams perform better.
  - Deployment pain signals poorer IT performance, organizational performance, and culture.
- **Metrics:** throughput and stability became the two factors ([metrics history](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-metrics-history/)).

## 2014 State of DevOps Report

- **URL:** <https://dora.dev/research/2014/> (with Puppet)
- **Headline findings** (from the [2014 page](https://dora.dev/research/2014/)):
  - High-performing IT organizations are twice as likely to exceed their profitability, market share, and productivity goals.
  - Version control and continuous delivery correlate with high IT performance. CI and automated testing are key practices.
  - Organizational culture strongly predicts IT and organizational performance. Job satisfaction is the top predictor of organizational
    performance.
  - IT performance keeps improving the longer an organization practises DevOps.
  - IT performance and organizational performance reinforce each other.
- **Metrics:** the first study. Change fail rate did not join the IT-performance construct that year
  ([metrics history](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-metrics-history/)).
